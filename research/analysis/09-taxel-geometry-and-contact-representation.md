## Tactile-covered hands in simulation - taxel geometry, contact representation, and its effect on manipulation

**Type:** papers

**Relevance:** HIGH - this cluster directly studies tactile-covered anthropomorphic hands, including XELA/uSkin-covered Allegro hands, simulated in Isaac Gym/IsaacLab/IsaacSim for dexterous manipulation with RL; however, none of the three gives a clean published recipe for LEAP-style protruding taxel collision geoms in MuJoCo.

### What it is

These three papers are the closest nearby published evidence for the LeapXELA plateau because they all study tactile sensing on anthropomorphic or multi-fingered hands for dexterous manipulation. Their relevance is not equal:

- `The Role of Touch` studies sensor placement on an Isaac Gym Shadow Hand reorientation/repositioning task. It is not XELA-specific and does not model discrete taxel collision geometry; it adds 19 force-torque sensing regions/sites over the hand.
- `Beyond Binary` studies a 16-DOF Allegro hand with XELA uSkin sensors covering fingertips, phalanges, and palm. It is the most explicit about converting between simulator contact quantities and uSkin-like taxel arrays. Its main contribution is a center-of-pressure representation derived from simulator contacts and mapped to/from raw taxel readings.
- `PTLD` studies an Allegro hand sensorized with Xela uSkin and explicitly avoids tactile simulation for deployment. It trains privileged policies in IsaacGym and distills real tactile histories into privileged latents. It is especially relevant because it includes in-hand reorientation and says that reorientation is infeasible in simulation without privileged/object-pose sensing.

For our debugging question, the strongest cross-paper signal is that these works do not present protruding per-taxel collision geometry as the standard RL training model. They either use force-torque sensing regions on an existing hand model, use simulator contact force/position on taxel-covered regions and then map those contacts to taxel readings, or bypass tactile simulation entirely during the deployable tactile phase.

### Key technical details

**Role of Touch (arXiv:2509.14984):**

- Simulator/task: Isaac Gym RL Shadow-Dexterous-Hand environment, derived from the OpenAI Gym robotics Shadow Hand model, calibrated in prior Shadow Hand work. The hand performs in-hand object reorientation/repositioning.
- Hand model: Shadow Dexterous Hand with 20 degrees of actuation; distal phalanges passively coupled with middle phalanges.
- Taxel/sensor modeling: the paper does not model XELA-like taxels. It divides the hand palm surface into 19 regions corresponding to the fingers, thumb phalanges, and five upper-palm regions. It says the model was enhanced with "19 sensing sites on specific body sub-parts" to measure average 3D force and torque in each segment volume.
- Collision geometry: no statement that the Shadow Hand collision geometry was changed when sensors were added. The text says forces and torques over each region are measured, not that regions become new collision bodies. It does not state separate taxel geoms, taxel dimensions, protrusion height, pad stiffness, pad compliance, or pad friction.
- Sensor count/coverage: 19 candidate hand regions. Experiments choose no sensors, 5 sensors, 8 sensors, or systematic region groupings. Baseline B2 uses 6D force-torque data from each of the 5 fingertips. The 8-sensor experiment fixes the 5 fingertips and varies 3 additional sensors among the remaining 14 spots.
- Contact representation: per selected region, 6D force-torque data. The paper measures "average 3D force and torque" per segment volume, with forces measured at the regions' center and torques in the local sensor frame. It does not derive raw taxels, center-of-pressure, or tactile latents from simulator contacts.
- Training setup: 4096 parallel Isaac Gym environments per experiment, maximum 3000 epochs, about 1 hour. PPO with an LSTM policy and an additional hidden layer with ReLU activations. Actions are desired joint angles relative to current ones. Observation baseline without touch is 52-dimensional and includes fingertip Cartesian positions, object pose, and relative-to-target orientation; touch expands the observation space and non-touch baseline uses zero-padding. Gaussian noise is included in object and fingertip position.
- Reward/task: success is correct orientation and position within a 0.1-radian margin before episode end and without dropping the object. Reward is `R = -alpha d - beta r - gamma ||a||^2 + S - F`, with distance, rotation disalignment, action penalty, success bonus, and failure penalty; the paper does not give the numeric scaling factors in the plaintext.
- Baseline results: tactile information improves success streak up to 26% and accelerates convergence by a factor of 3. The fingertip group gets 28.21 consecutive successes and 3.2x convergence speed; upper palm gets 27.87 and 3.1x; middle phalanges get 25.89 and 2.7x; proximal phalanges get 25.77 and 2.2x.
- Placement ablation: in 5-sensor tennis-ball experiments, best normalized individual region is THpalm = 1.000, then RFdist = 0.882, MFpalm = 0.833, THdis = 0.813. Worst is FFmid = 0.000, then RFmid = 0.047, MFmid = 0.106. The best tested 5-sensor configuration included the whole thumb plus ring and middle distal phalanges. With 5 fingertips plus 3 extra sensors, FFmid = 1.000, LFpalm = 0.747, FFprox = 0.730, THprox = 0.688; RFpalm = 0.000 is worst.
- Object-size ablation: default sphere is 7 cm diameter. Larger sphere is 10 cm, smaller sphere is 4 cm. Failure rate is around 4% for small spheres and 8% for larger spheres. The range of sensor-placement effect is 24.2% of the lowest value for larger objects and 41.9% for smaller objects. For larger spheres, TH, FF, and MF dominate; for smaller spheres, the middle finger base is most important and the best configuration uses five palm locations.
- Object-shape ablation: ellipsoid and cube, similar volume to the tennis-ball sphere. Tactile sensing improves all tested geometries, and the best sphere-derived configurations surpass fingertip-only B2 for ellipsoid and cube.
- Stiffness/friction/compliance: the paper does not report tactile pad stiffness, tactile pad friction, or compliance values. It explicitly says its model abstracts "the precise, often deformable, contact mechanics of tactile sensors."
- Training failures/plateaus: no plateau resembling our 145-170 reward failure is described. It reports convergence-speed improvements, not failed late takeoff. It says tactile sensing improves performance and learning efficiency, but this does not isolate collision geometry because sensing is added without reported collision-geometry changes.
- Smooth collision recommendation: no explicit recommendation to keep collision geometry smooth and sensing separate. The implementation implicitly separates sensing from collision by adding sensing sites/regions to an existing hand model rather than adding protruding taxel collision geoms, but the paper does not state this as a design rule.

**Beyond Binary (arXiv:2605.28812):**

- Simulator/task: IsaacLab/IsaacSim sim-to-real RL on a 16-DOF Allegro hand with XELA uSkin sensors covering fingertips, phalanges, and palm. Two blind tasks: peg-in-hole insertion and ball balancing. The actor observes proprioception plus contact representation; the critic additionally observes privileged task state.
- Taxel/sensor modeling: the paper assumes uSkin-like arrays where each sensor array has `N` taxels in a grid-like arrangement and each taxel is an individual 3-axis force measurement point. It does not state the value of `N` per array, the number of uSkin pads, total taxel count, taxel dimensions, taxel protrusion, or taxel spacing in the plaintext.
- Collision/contact modeling: the simulation does not appear to use one collision geom per taxel. The paper uses IsaacLab's `ContactSensor` API to track contacts between "taxel-covered regions of each fingertip link" and the contact object. Appendix B says grey boxes represent the taxel-covered regions used to filter contacts during simulation. This implies region-level collision/contact filtering, not individual protruding taxel collision geoms, but the paper does not fully specify the underlying collision mesh/primitive geometry or whether it was changed from the base Allegro model.
- Simulator contact quantities: the CoP representation starts from standard simulator pairwise contacts. The paper states that rigid-body simulators such as IsaacSim and MuJoCo provide pairwise contact information including 3D contact force vector and contact position in world frame.
- Contact representation: CoP is a 6D local descriptor per contact region: a 3D contact force vector and a 3D Cartesian contact position, both in the sensor frame. A footnote clarifies it is a resultant contact wrench reduced to a single force vector at a centroidal contact point, not a full multi-contact pressure distribution.
- Taxel-CoP mapping: real raw taxel readings are mapped into CoP. CoP position is estimated as a force-magnitude-weighted average of active taxel positions, where the active set is taxels with force norm above threshold epsilon. Taxel normals are used to account for curved fingertip geometry. The stress distribution model includes normal/shear decomposition, a Gaussian radial spread hyperparameter `sigma`, a blended direction between taxel normal and vector from CoP to taxel, and regularized least-squares with regularization `lambda` to recover the CoP force.
- Shear limitation: because IsaacLab contact estimates for their fingertip geometries produced unreliable shear components, they use only the surface-normal component of CoP in both simulation and hardware. This is an explicit simulator-contact instability/mismatch, but it is about shear estimation rather than per-taxel collision geometry degrading PPO.
- Calibration: taxel origins come from sensor specifications, but taxel rotations are learned because they are hard to manually calibrate on a complex curved fingertip. Appendix A collects 2400 samples, equal to 2 minutes at 20 Hz, of random object-fingertip contacts. It uses Adam with learning rate 0.1 and 100 full-batch gradient steps to learn taxel orientations.
- Actuation/contact dynamics: actuator dynamics are aligned in IsaacSim by Bayesian optimization over actuator stiffness, damping, and joint friction. The paper notes subtle actuator dynamics such as non-uniform friction and gear backlash are difficult to simulate and can significantly degrade performance.
- Sensor delay: tactile delay is explicitly measured and added during simulation training. Domain randomization lists joint position observation delay of 0.05 s and contact observation delay in [0.05, 0.1] s.
- Policy/action: actor observes joint angles, previous actions, and contact representation. Actions are 16-D target joint position increments, clipped to [-1, 1]^16. Action scale is 0.03 for insertion and 0.05 for balancing. EMA alpha is 0.5. PD gains are P = 3.0, D = 0.1 for insertion and P = 6.0, D = 0.15 for balancing.
- PPO: asymmetric actor-critic PPO with online observation normalization. Adaptive learning rate starts at 5.0e-4 with target KL 0.016. Each iteration collects 64 steps per environment for insertion and 16 for balancing, then performs 5 epochs over 4 minibatches. Discount gamma = 0.99, GAE lambda = 0.95, clip range = 0.2, entropy coefficient = 0.005. Training curves are over 5 random seeds.
- Domain randomization, insertion: peg mass [0.03, 0.04] kg; peg static/dynamic friction [0.2, 0.4]/[0.1, 0.2]; peg roll/pitch U(-0.2, 0.2) rad; peg yaw U(0, 2pi). Hole static/dynamic friction [0.3, 0.5]/[0.1, 0.3]. Hand static/dynamic friction [0.5, 0.7]/[0.3, 0.5]. Hand initial position U(-0.5, 0.5) cm, roll/pitch U(-0.02, 0.02) rad, DOF U(-0.05, 0.05) rad.
- Domain randomization, balancing: ball mass [0.05, 0.25] kg; ball static/dynamic friction [0.01, 0.02]/[0.0, 0.01]; ball initial position U(-5.0, 5.0) cm. Plate mass [0.035, 0.055] kg; plate friction [0.01, 0.02]/[0.0, 0.01]. Hand static/dynamic friction [1.9, 2.0]/[1.8, 1.9]. General PD P multiplied by U(0.8, 1.2), D by U(0.7, 1.3). Force-vector observation noise probability 0.2, rotation U(-0.1, 0.1) rad, magnitude U(0.9, 1.1). Contact-position noise probability 0.2, noise U(-0.1, 0.1) cm.
- Rewards: insertion has goal distance reward weight 1.0, one-time goal reached reward weight 400.0, good contact reward `sum_i 1(||f_i|| >= 1.0)` weight 0.25, rotation deviation penalty weight 1.0, hand DOF position deviation penalty weight 1.0. Ball balancing has goal distance reward weight 1.0, plate contact reward `sum_i 1(||f_i|| >= 0)` weight 0.2, ball-plate relative velocity penalty 1.0, plate position deviation penalty 1.0, plate yaw penalty 1.0, ball fallen penalty 200.0, action diff penalty 1.0.
- Real insertion results: CoP has the highest overall success rate, 0.78, with mean time 10.34 +/- 7.62 s. Baselines: base 0.43, binary 0.53, magnitude 0.55, vector 0.67, position 0.50, raw taxel 0.48. Under out-of-distribution initialization, CoP success is 0.63 versus base 0.17, binary 0.20, magnitude 0.27, vector 0.42, position 0.28, raw taxel 0.27. Under 40% masked raw hardware taxel forces, CoP success is 0.62; binary is 0.52; raw taxel is 0.30.
- Real ball-balancing results: CoP has overall time-to-fall 4.60 +/- 2.19 s. Vector-only is 4.52 +/- 2.93 s. Base is 1.38 +/- 0.21, binary 1.99 +/- 1.03, magnitude 2.40 +/- 0.79, position 1.55 +/- 0.27, raw taxel 1.49 +/- 0.36. Human overall is 9.37 +/- 5.32.
- Training/failure modes: in ball balancing, policies lacking explicit force information - base, binary, and position-only - completely fail to learn in simulation. Raw taxel observations perform worse than compact contact representations, attributed to high dimensionality and complexity of raw taxel forces. In insertion, raw taxel performs worse than nearly all baselines, attributed to imperfect tactile simulation, higher dimensionality, and sensor-specific mismatch. Simplified binary/contact representations can make fast initial insertions but fail to adapt to peg-hole contacts and OOD initialization.
- Collision degradation: no explicit statement that adding taxel collision geometry degraded or destabilized training. The explicit degradation statements are about unreliable simulated shear, imperfect tactile simulation/raw-taxel mismatch, hard-to-simulate actuator dynamics/friction/backlash, and jamming from low insertion tolerance caused by mesh approximations and collision-resolution numerical errors.
- Smooth collision recommendation: no direct sentence saying to keep collision smooth and separate from sensing. However, the method is strongly consistent with that practice: it gets policy observations from simulator contact force/position on taxel-covered regions, then converts those compact physical contacts to/from raw taxel readings using a separate differentiable sensing model. It does not require individual taxels to be collision bodies.

**PTLD (arXiv:2603.04531):**

- Simulator/task: IsaacGym for simulation; Allegro hand with Xela uSkin attached to a Franka Panda. Tasks include z-axis in-hand rotation and in-hand reorientation. Real deployment runs at about 20 Hz.
- Hardware sensing: 18 Xela uSkin sensing pads cover the Allegro hand, totaling 368 individual sensors. The tactile observation shape is 368 x 3. The authors use 3-axis raw sensor measurements instead of processed force measurements because processed force measurements have significant hysteresis and lag. A 2-minute no-contact baseline is subtracted before each data-collection run.
- Taxel/sensor geometry in simulation: PTLD deliberately avoids simulating tactile sensors for the deployable tactile policy. It trains a privileged policy in simulation with object pose/shape and other privileged information, deploys that policy in a real instrumented cell, collects paired real tactile observations and privileged-policy latents, then trains a tactile encoder to match the privileged latent. Therefore, there are no simulated per-taxel collision geoms, no simulated taxel dimensions, no protrusion heights, and no tactile-pad stiffness/compliance/friction values stated for sensor geometry.
- Underlying collision geometry: not specified in the plaintext beyond "Xela hand" / "Allegro hand sensorized with Xela uSkin" and use of IsaacGym. It does not say whether the simulated Allegro collision geometry includes Xela pad geometry or is changed to match the real sensorized hand. It does say they generate a grasp cache tuned for the Xela hand, retaining grasps if the object is held stably after 50 simulation steps, but gives no collision-shape construction details.
- Contact representation: not a simulator contact representation for the deployable policy. For rotation, the tactile encoder input concatenates tactile observations `X_tactile in R^(368 x 3)` and tactile sensor positions from forward kinematics `X_sensor_pos in R^(368 x 3)`, forming `R^(368 x 6)`. The tactile observations are produced at 100 Hz, and the encoder uses 0.5 s of tactile history. The encoder is MLP -> temporal convolution -> MLP -> latent `z`.
- Reorientation tactile representation: a causal Transformer recursively predicts future latents. It embeds tactile observations `R^(368 x 3)`, proprioception `R^32`, goal orientations, and previous latent `z_t in R^8` via individual 2-layer MLPs; these are concatenated, projected with positional encodings, and processed by a Transformer decoder.
- Privileged simulation representation: in reorientation, the adaptation module during simulation receives Allegro joint states `q_t in R^16`, previous targets `q_tilde_t in R^16`, noisy object pose `(p_t, R_t)`, and goal orientations. Orientations use 6D representations. Relative pose difference to goal is concatenated. A 30-step observation history, about 1.5 s at 20 Hz, is frame-stacked. The privileged encoder further receives object velocities and full fingertip states, including poses and velocities.
- Reorientation goal/task: goal is object pose within delta <= 0.25 rad of target. Goals are sampled in the upper hemisphere, theta = 40 degrees from the z-axis. Additional results say simulation uses a wider goal-pose distribution of about 40 degrees about z and a tighter success threshold of 20 degrees; real-world threshold is relaxed to about 30 degrees.
- Reward, reorientation: `r_goal = 1 / (d(R_object_t, R_goal_t) + epsilon)` scale 2.0; `r_success` when orientation distance <= delta scale 5.0; `r_streak = N_success/N_max_success` scale 2.0; `r_contact = sum_i(C_i > delta_contact)` scale 0.1; `r_position = ||p_t - p_0||` scale 0.05; `r_finger_pose = ||q_t - q_0||` scale -1.0; `r_fingertip_object = sum_i ||p_fingertip_i - p_t||` scale -0.2. Penalties include angular velocity -0.05, acceleration -0.005, action -0.005, action rate -0.2, joint limit -0.1, object velocity -1.0, torque -0.5, work -4.0, timeout -1.0, alive -0.01.
- Reset/curriculum-like details: starts each episode from a stable grasp sampled from a grasp set. Goal orientations reset multiple times within an episode whenever the object reaches the goal. Episode resets on z-height threshold when the object drops or slips into unrecoverable states, plus standard fixed-step reset. Stable grasp generation starts from a canonical Xela-hand pose, randomizes joint configuration within limits, drops/places the object slightly above the hand, and retains grasps if stable after 50 simulation steps.
- Training/data: simulation uses PPO with AAC and online latent distillation. After collecting real-world offline data, the tactile encoder is trained with AdamW, LR = 1e-4. For rotation tactile policy, they collect about 180 minutes of real-world rotation trajectory data: 30 trajectories of 2 minutes each per DAgger iteration, 3 DAgger rounds total.
- Rotation results: simulation baseline Table 1 shows Oracle RotR 159.4, TTF 0.89, RotP 31.86; RMA proprioception 139.0, 0.79, 28.53; RMA + pose 153.1, 0.86, 31.09; AAC proprioception 141.9, 0.80, 27.83; AAC + pose 168.6, 0.88, 26.5. Cube real-world Table 3 shows AAC Proprio+Pose RotR 17.3 +/- 8.8, TTF 0.33 +/- 0.15, RotP 5.5 +/- 3.6; Proprio distillation 0.5 +/- 0.5, 0.11 +/- 0.03, 1.3 +/- 0.9; Tactile distillation 4.5 +/- 2.3, 0.34 +/- 0.21, 50.8 +/- 28.1.
- Reorientation results: Table 4 reports Transformer proprioception reaches 2.1 +/- 2.14 goals with TTF 10.99 +/- 10.15 s; Transformer tactile+proprioception reaches 3.3 +/- 1.55 goals with TTF 13.42 +/- 10.18 s, a 57% improvement in number of goals reached as stated in the abstract.
- Failure modes: the abstract and contributions say tactile in-hand reorientation is a task that cannot be trained in simulation using proprioceptive history alone. Section 5.2 says reorientation is infeasible to learn in simulation without privileged sensors/object pose. Section 6.4 says temporal-convolutional encoders often collapse into uni-directional gaiting, while the autoregressive Transformer captures long-range dependencies for multi-directional reorientation. For cube rotation, proprioceptive baselines fail almost immediately, while tactile signals distinguish edge and face contact and enable corrections.
- Stiffness/friction/compliance: PTLD does not report tactile pad stiffness, pad compliance, or pad friction. It does report object diversity with varying surface frictions, cylinder radii 30 mm, 31.75 mm twice, and 33.4 mm; heights 70 mm, 178 mm twice, and 200 mm; square bottle side lengths 54 mm and 60 mm with heights 187 mm and 194 mm; cube edges 64 mm and 80 mm; masses 22 g, 24 g, 30 g, 46 g, 87 g, 90 g, plus optional 18 g and 36 g inserted weights made from 9 g pieces.
- Collision degradation: no explicit statement that adding sensor collision geometry degraded or destabilized training. The paper's premise is that fast, realistic tactile simulation is lacking and that tactile simulation packages are not standardized and rely on rigid-body simulation, compounding accuracy problems. It bypasses tactile simulation rather than diagnosing protruding-taxel contact instability.
- Smooth collision recommendation: no explicit recommendation to keep collision smooth and sensing separate. But the central design is an even stronger separation: train with privileged non-tactile simulation state and learn real tactile-to-latent mapping outside simulation, so deployable tactile sensing is not produced by simulated taxel collision geometry at all.

**Synthesis:**

- None of the three papers states that individual taxels should be modeled as separate protruding collision geoms for RL training. The closest XELA/uSkin works do not provide evidence that per-taxel protruding collision is standard practice.
- The strongest positive practice signal is separation of sensing from collision/contact. `Role of Touch` adds force-torque sensing sites/regions to an existing Shadow Hand model. `Beyond Binary` reads simulator contact force and contact position on taxel-covered regions and separately maps between CoP and raw taxels. `PTLD` bypasses tactile simulation entirely for deployable tactile policies and distills real tactile histories into privileged latents.
- There is no direct paper statement saying "keep collision smooth." Still, all three avoid the exact failure-prone design we are worried about: changing the finger collision surface into many small taxel protrusions and training PPO through that contact model.
- The papers do give direct warnings around contact representation. `Beyond Binary` says raw taxel observations underperform compact CoP/vector representations because of imperfect tactile simulation, higher dimensionality, and sensor-specific mismatch. It also says simulated shear components were unreliable for their fingertip geometries, so they restrict to normal-force CoP for robustness. `PTLD` says realistic tactile simulation is a core issue and bypasses it.
- The placement result from `Role of Touch` matters for future tactile collection but not for the current pure-RL plateau. It says distributed sensing outside fingertips helps manipulation, but those sensors are observation channels, not collision-shape changes.
- The reward/reset result from `PTLD` is highly relevant to our plateau: it uses stable grasp starts, repeated goal resets within an episode, a success/streak structure, explicit contact/object-centering incentives, and strong penalties against unrecoverable finger curling. Our environment starts from a grasp/manipulation setting where the policy can stably hold the cube but may not encounter the sparse success bonus often enough. PTLD's multi-goal reset/streak design directly attacks that.

### How it differs from our setup

Our current `LeapXELACubeReorient` setup changes the LEAP Hand collision geometry to include XELA taxel arrays on fingertips/phalanges, while tactile observations are currently ignored. This is exactly the combination the three papers do not validate: extra collision complexity from tactile hardware without using touch as a policy input.

`Role of Touch` uses a Shadow Hand, not LEAP/LeapXELA; Isaac Gym, not MuJoCo/MJX; a sphere/ellipsoid/cube manipulation setup, not specifically the MuJoCo Playground LEAP cube reorientation task; and force-torque sensing regions, not XELA taxel collision geometry. It gives sensor placement evidence, but no evidence that adding physical taxel bumps to collision is harmless.

`Beyond Binary` uses XELA uSkin on Allegro and is close in spirit, but its simulator contact model is built around region-level contact filtering and CoP, not per-taxel protruding MuJoCo collision geoms. It also uses tactile/contact observations in the actor, whereas our current debug setup ignores touch. Its action increments are much smaller than ours: 0.03/0.05 with EMA alpha 0.5 versus our action_scale 0.5 and ema_alpha 1.0. Its hand friction ranges are also task-specific and sometimes much higher: insertion hand static/dynamic [0.5, 0.7]/[0.3, 0.5], balancing [1.9, 2.0]/[1.8, 1.9], while our fingertip friction randomization is U(0.5, 1.0) and may be hitting the wrong geoms because the actual taxel contacts may be named `<finger>_tip_1..16`.

`PTLD` uses Xela uSkin on Allegro and includes in-hand reorientation, but it does not train a deployable tactile policy from simulated tactile contacts. It uses privileged object pose in simulation, then real tactile distillation. Our actor does not observe current cube orientation directly; it observes noisy cube orientation error/history. PTLD explicitly says current object orientation is essential for policy success in their reorientation setup. PTLD's reorientation uses 30-step history, about 1.5 s at 20 Hz, while ours uses `history_len=1`. PTLD starts from stable grasp caches and resets goals multiple times inside an episode on success; our setup follows the stock environment and only gets the large `success_reward=100` when success happens, which rarely occurs in the plateau.

### What it says about our plateau

The papers do not prove that XELA collision geometry is the cause of our plateau, but they make our current design look unusually risky. The closest XELA/uSkin papers either use contact-region CoP or bypass tactile simulation, not hundreds of protruding taxel collision features. If our LeapXELA geometry turns each taxel into a contact-producing geom, then the policy is learning through a rougher, higher-contact-count finger surface while receiving no tactile benefit from that extra geometry.

The plateau at 145-170 reward, interpreted in `CONTEXT.md` as stable holding without success-threshold crossings, is consistent with several source-backed failure patterns:

- `Beyond Binary` shows coarse/binary or insufficient contact representations can perform fast initial attempts but fail to adapt to secondary contacts and OOD initializations. Our actor has no tactile/contact representation at all, and the XELA collision geometry may make contact transitions more complex than the stock LEAP hand.
- `PTLD` says in-hand reorientation cannot be trained in simulation using proprioceptive history alone and is infeasible without privileged sensors/object pose in their setup. Our actor has cube goal/error observations, so it is not purely proprioceptive, but it lacks long temporal history, tactile force/contact geometry, and privileged velocities/fingertip states available to the critic only.
- `PTLD` reports temporal-convolutional encoders collapse into uni-directional gaiting for reorientation, while an autoregressive Transformer is needed for multi-directional reorientation. Our MLP with one-step history may be able to hold but not develop robust multi-step reorientation gaits under the harder XELA contact surface.
- `Beyond Binary` explicitly reports that simulated shear estimates were unreliable for their fingertip geometries and that raw taxel observations are harmed by imperfect tactile simulation, higher dimensionality, and sensor-specific mismatch. This strengthens suspicion that detailed tactile-surface simulation can introduce misleading contact mechanics unless carefully abstracted/calibrated.
- `Beyond Binary` also reports jamming when insertion tolerance is zero or low, due to mesh approximations and possible collision-resolution numerical errors. That is not our task, but it is direct evidence that small geometric/contact tolerances in simulation can create manipulation failures unrelated to the policy's high-level objective.

The most actionable interpretation is: first test whether the XELA collision shape alone is the blocker. Published tactile-hand RL work does not require protruding taxels to be collision geoms, and it often abstracts sensing away from raw tactile geometry. Therefore, a smooth-collision LeapXELA variant with the same inertial/visual/sensor frames but smooth fingertip collision would be a high-value ablation.

### Concrete things to try

1. Make a smooth-collision LeapXELA ablation: keep XELA visuals/sites/inertials as needed, but make the contacting fingertip/phalange collision surfaces smooth and LEAP-like. Do not remove the future tactile sensor frames; just prevent each taxel bump from contributing separate contact geometry. This is the closest test of the cross-paper standard practice.

2. Make a "collision-only taxels, no sensing" versus "smooth collision, taxel sensing sites" paired run. The current setup is effectively collision-only taxels because touch is ignored. The papers suggest sensing can be useful, but collision roughness without observation benefit is not supported.

3. Verify friction randomization hits the actual contacting geoms. `CONTEXT.md` already notes fingertip randomization may target `th_tip/if_tip/mf_tip/rf_tip` while real contacts may be on `<finger>_tip_1..16`. If taxel geoms remain active, assign friction consistently across them; if smooth collision is used, make sure the smooth contacting geoms receive the intended U(0.5, 1.0) or a controlled fixed value.

4. Add contact-budget telemetry rather than only increasing `nconmax/njmax`. Log max/mean contacts, constraints, and dropped/overflow events per rollout/eval, especially contacts per finger/object. `Beyond Binary` and PTLD do not give `njmax/nconmax`, but they both avoid high-dimensional raw taxel contact simulation for policy learning; if taxels multiply contacts, saturation or noisy contact manifolds could produce exactly a stable-hold/no-rotate regime.

5. Reduce action aggressiveness for a diagnostic run. `Beyond Binary` uses target-position increments scaled by 0.03 or 0.05 with EMA alpha 0.5. Our action scale is 0.5 with EMA alpha 1.0. The stock LEAP baseline works with this, but the rougher XELA contact surface may make large integrating target jumps harder to explore. Try smaller action scales such as 0.05 or 0.1 and/or EMA smoothing.

6. Try PTLD-like reorientation scaffolding: stable grasp cache starts, multiple goal resets per episode, success/streak reward, and explicit contact/object-centering shaping. PTLD's reorientation reward includes success and streak bonuses plus contact and centering terms, and it resets goals repeatedly within an episode. This directly targets the sparse-success reachability problem in our plateau.

7. Increase temporal context for the actor. PTLD uses 30-step history, about 1.5 s at 20 Hz, for reorientation and finds recurrent/Transformer structure important for multi-directional behavior. Beyond Binary finds a GRU more sample-efficient and better-converged than MLP histories. A short diagnostic could be `history_len > 1`; a stronger change would be recurrent policy support.

8. If/when tactile observations are introduced, do not feed raw taxels first. The most source-backed representations are 6D force-torque region features (`Role of Touch`), CoP/contact force plus contact position (`Beyond Binary`), or a learned latent from real tactile histories (`PTLD`). `Beyond Binary` shows raw taxel observations underperform compact physical representations.

9. If using CoP-like observations in MuJoCo/MJX, derive them from simulator contacts on smooth fingertip/region geoms: total/normal force and contact centroid per region, expressed in a local sensor frame. This mirrors `Beyond Binary`'s "simulator contact -> CoP -> taxel mapping" separation and avoids needing taxel-by-taxel collision contacts.

10. Test fixed friction/stiffness/contact parameters before broad randomization. `Beyond Binary` treats actuator stiffness, damping, joint friction, object friction, hand friction, observation delay, force noise, and contact-position noise as critical. Our randomization may be partly misapplied under renamed taxel geoms. A fixed, known-contact-geom run can separate reward/exploration failure from randomization mismatch.
